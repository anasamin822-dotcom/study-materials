Unit 1 - Atomic & Molecular Structure

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): atomic theory and nuclear models, Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, de Broglie waves, the Schrödinger equation for hydrogen, VBT and MOT of diatomic molecules (H₂, O₂, N₂, H₂O, NH₃, XeO₃, XeO₄), crystal field theory and magnetic properties of transition metal ions, and band theory of metals.

Key ideas: de Broglie: every moving particle has a wave, λ = h/mv. Heisenberg: Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π - position and momentum cannot both be exact. The Schrödinger equation (Hψ = Eψ) gives orbitals as solutions. VBT: bonds form by orbital overlap, explained with hybridisation (sp³ in NH₃/H₂O). MOT: atomic orbitals combine into bonding (lower energy) and antibonding (higher) molecular orbitals; bond order = (bonding - antibonding electrons)/2 - O₂ has bond order 2 and is paramagnetic (two unpaired electrons), the classic question. Crystal field theory: d-orbitals split in a ligand field (t₂g/eg in octahedral); strong-field ligands pair electrons (low spin), weak-field leave them unpaired (high spin) - this decides magnetism. Band theory: metals conduct because their valence and conduction bands overlap.

Exam pointers: MO diagram of O₂ or N₂ with bond order and magnetism is the most repeated question. de Broglie/Heisenberg numericals (plug into the formula) and "VBT vs MOT" are regular theory.

Unit 2 - Thermodynamics & Electrochemistry

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): first and second laws of thermodynamics; energy, entropy and free energy; EMF and cell potential; the Nernst equation and applications; acids and bases; Ellingham diagrams in metallurgy; Hess's law.

Key ideas: First law: energy is conserved - ΔU = q + w. Second law: entropy of the universe increases for a spontaneous process. Gibbs free energy ΔG = ΔH - TΔS decides spontaneity: ΔG < 0 spontaneous. Electrochemistry: a galvanic cell converts chemical energy to electricity; EMF = E(cathode) - E(anode), and ΔG = -nFE connects the two halves of the unit. The Nernst equation E = E° - (0.0591/n) log Q (at 25°C) corrects for concentration - the standard numerical. Hess's law: total enthalpy change is the same whatever the path - add/subtract given equations to reach the target. Ellingham diagram plots ΔG° vs T for oxide formation; the line lower at a given temperature reduces the oxide above it - this explains choice of reducing agents in metallurgy.

Exam pointers: one Nernst-equation numerical (calculate EMF at given concentrations), ΔG = ΔH - TΔS spontaneity checks, and a Hess's law calculation are the regulars. "Explain the Ellingham diagram and its use in metallurgy" is a frequent long answer.

Unit 3 - Water Chemistry & Corrosion

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): hardness of water and its treatment (Clark's, lime-soda, zeolite, reverse osmosis), industrial water treatment, alkalinity, and corrosion - theories, types, mechanism and control.

Key ideas: Hardness: temporary (bicarbonates of Ca/Mg - removed by boiling or Clark's method with lime) and permanent (chlorides/sulphates - need lime-soda, zeolite or ion exchange). The lime-soda process adds Ca(OH)₂ and Na₂CO₃ to precipitate CaCO₃ and Mg(OH)₂; the zeolite process swaps Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ for Na⁺ (regenerated with brine); reverse osmosis pushes water through a membrane against osmotic pressure. Hardness is expressed as CaCO₃ equivalents: mg/L = (mass of salt x 100 x 1000)/(MW of salt x volume). Corrosion = metal eaten by its environment: the electrochemical theory sets up tiny anode-cathode cells on the metal surface (rusting: Fe oxidises at the anode, O₂ reduces at the cathode). Types: galvanic, pitting, stress, water-line. Control: coatings, galvanisation (sacrificial zinc), cathodic protection, inhibitors.

Exam pointers: hardness numericals (convert to CaCO₃ equivalents, or lime/soda requirement) appear almost every year - practise the formula. "Explain the electrochemical theory of rusting with the anode/cathode reactions" and "compare lime-soda and zeolite" are the standard theory answers.

Unit 4 - Materials: Polymers, Fuels, Lubricants, Glass

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): materials - polymers, fuels, lubricants and glass/cement basics.

Key ideas: Polymers: addition (chain-growth - polyethylene, PVC) vs condensation (step-growth - nylon, bakelite, with a small molecule eliminated). Thermoplastics soften on heating (recyclable); thermosets set permanently. Fuels: calorific value (higher and lower), characteristics of a good fuel, knocking and octane number for petrol, cetane number for diesel; gaseous fuels (CNG, LPG, biogas) compared on calorific value. Lubricants: reduce friction and wear; classified solid/semi-solid/liquid with the key properties (viscosity, viscosity index, flash point) - full details in the notes written under this unit. Glass: a supercooled liquid of silicates; types (soda-lime, borosilicate) and annealing.

Exam pointers: "addition vs condensation polymerisation with two examples each" and "thermoplastic vs thermoset" tables are free marks. Octane vs cetane numbers and "properties of a good lubricant" are the short-answer regulars.

Lubricants (properties & classification)
Lubricants: Functions & Classification· Dr. Anjali Saxena - Hindi
✎ Lubricants - quick exam notes (Instinct) - no video found, taught here+

Lubricant: a substance between two moving surfaces that reduces friction and wear.

Functions: reduces friction and wear, carries away heat, seals out dust, prevents corrosion, and cushions shock between parts.

Classification: (1) Solid - graphite, molybdenum disulphide (used where oils would catch fire or leak); (2) Semi-solid - greases (oil + soap thickener; for slow, heavy loads); (3) Liquid - mineral oils (petroleum), vegetable and animal oils, blended oils (mineral oil + additives for viscosity index, oxidation stability).

Key properties: viscosity and viscosity index (how much viscosity changes with temperature), flash and fire point, cloud and pour point (low-temperature flow), oiliness (sticking to the surface), acid value, saponification value.

Exam tip: definitions + one example each of solid/semi-solid/liquid lubricant, and the meaning of viscosity index - this pattern repeats in papers.

Lubricant: do moving surfaces ke beech ka aisa padarth jo friction aur wear (ghisav) kam kare.

Functions: friction kam karna, heat hatana, dust seal karna, corrosion rokna, aur shock absorb karna.

Classification: (1) Solid - graphite, molybdenum disulphide (jahan oil jal sakta ho ya leak ho); (2) Semi-solid - grease (oil + soap thickener; slow, heavy load ke liye); (3) Liquid - mineral oils (petroleum), vegetable/animal oils, blended oils (additives ke saath).

Key properties: viscosity aur viscosity index (temperature se viscosity kitna badalta hai), flash/fire point, cloud/pour point (thande me flow), oiliness (surface se chipakna).

Unit 5 - Spectroscopy, Organic Reactions & Drug Synthesis

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): spectroscopy (principle, selection rules, electronic/vibrational/rotational), NMR and MRI, organic reaction types (addition, elimination, substitution, cyclization - Diels-Alder), and synthesis of one drug (aspirin, phenacetin, melubrin, novalgin).

Key ideas: Spectroscopy studies how molecules absorb electromagnetic radiation: rotational (microwave), vibrational (infrared), electronic (UV-visible) - energy gaps decide the region. Selection rules say which transitions are allowed (vibrational needs a changing dipole moment; rotational needs a permanent dipole). IR is used to identify functional groups from characteristic stretching frequencies. NMR: nuclei with spin (¹H) flip between spin states in a magnetic field; chemical shift tells the proton's environment, splitting (n+1 rule) tells the neighbours. MRI applies the same physics to image body tissue - see the simple notes under this unit. Organic reactions: addition (across double bonds), elimination (form double bonds), substitution (swap a group), and the Diels-Alder cycloaddition (diene + dienophile → six-membered ring). Aspirin synthesis: salicylic acid + acetic anhydride (esterification of the -OH group).

Exam pointers: "principle of NMR spectroscopy" and "application of IR" are the common theory questions; one Diels-Alder mechanism and the aspirin synthesis (equation with conditions) appear regularly. Draw the MO-free, arrow-pushing steps cleanly - mechanism marks are step marks.

MRI (application)
✎ MRI - made simple (Instinct) - no video found, taught here+

MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging) = a machine that photographs the inside of the body, working on the principle of NMR spectroscopy.

Just as NMR finds the structure of small molecules by applying a magnetic field, MRI maps the whole body (mostly the hydrogen atoms in water).
  • Hydrogen (in water) is everywhere in the body. A strong magnetic field + radio waves are applied.
  • Hydrogen in each tissue gives a different signal - a computer turns that into an image.
  • Advantage: no harmful radiation (unlike X-rays), and a clear image of soft tissue.

Exam tip: "MRI is an application of NMR" - do not forget to write this one line.

MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging) = body ke andar ki photo kheenchne ki machine, jo NMR spectroscopy ke principle pe kaam karti hai.

QUESTION OF THE DAY

One exam question, every day

A new question rotates in each day - try it before you open the answer.

❓ Using MOT, find the bond order of O2 and state whether it is paramagnetic or diamagnetic.+
O2 has 16 electrons. Filling the MOs: (sigma1s)^2 (sigma*1s)^2 (sigma2s)^2 (sigma*2s)^2 (sigma2pz)^2 (pi2p)^4 (pi*2p)^2. Bond order = (10 - 6)/2 = 2. The two pi* electrons occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins (Hund's rule) - so O2 is PARAMAGNETIC.
❓ State the Nernst equation for a single electrode at 25 C and calculate E for a Zn2+/Zn electrode, E0 = -0.76 V, [Zn2+] = 0.1 M.+
E = E0 - (0.0591/n) log(1/[Mn+]) = E0 + (0.0591/n) log[Mn+]. For Zn, n = 2: E = -0.76 + (0.0591/2) log(0.1) = -0.76 + 0.0296 x (-1) = -0.7896 V, about -0.79 V.
❓ Distinguish temporary and permanent hardness of water, with one removal method for each.+
Temporary: caused by bicarbonates of Ca/Mg, removed by BOILING (or Clark's method - adding lime). Permanent: caused by chlorides/sulphates of Ca/Mg, NOT removed by boiling - needs lime-soda, zeolite, or ion-exchange treatment.
❓ Write the synthesis of aspirin with the reagents and the type of reaction.+
Salicylic acid is heated with acetic anhydride (a drop of conc. H2SO4 as catalyst): the -OH group of salicylic acid is acetylated to -OCOCH3, giving acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin). Reaction type: esterification (acetylation).
CHEAT SHEET

Last-night revision

Key formulas and facts in one list - scan it the night before the paper.

  • Atomic structure: orbitals fill by Aufbau order (1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d...), Pauli exclusion (max 2 e- per orbital, opposite spins), Hund's rule (fill singly first).
  • Molecular orbital theory: bonding (lower energy) vs antibonding* orbitals; bond order = (bonding e- − antibonding e-)/2.
  • Thermodynamics: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; ΔG < 0 → spontaneous.
  • First law: ΔU = q + w (work done on the system).
  • Electrochemistry: E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode; positive E°cell → spontaneous. Nernst: E = E° − (0.0591/n)log Q at 25°C.
  • Corrosion = electrochemical: anodic metal oxidises (rusts). Prevention: coating, galvanisation (Zn), sacrificial anode, alloying (stainless steel).
  • Water hardness: temporary = bicarbonates of Ca/Mg, removed by boiling; permanent = chlorides/sulphates, removed by washing soda / ion exchange / EDTA.
  • Hardness is reported as CaCO3 equivalent (ppm). EDTA titration estimates it with Eriochrome Black T indicator.
  • Polymers: addition (e.g. polyethylene, PVC) vs condensation (e.g. nylon, bakelite). Thermoplastics soften on heating; thermosets do not.
  • Fuels: calorific value = heat per unit mass. CNG/LPG are cleaner than coal/petrol. Octane number (petrol) and cetane number (diesel) rate knocking behaviour.
  • Lubricants: reduce friction and wear; key properties - viscosity, viscosity index, flash point, pour point.
  • Glass: supercooled liquid of silicates; amorphous, softens over a range instead of melting sharply.
  • Spectroscopy: UV-Vis (electronic transitions), IR (bond vibrations - identifies functional groups), NMR (nuclear environment).
  • Common name reactions: substitution (SN1 two-step via carbocation, SN2 one-step backside attack), elimination vs substitution, oxidation of alcohols → aldehydes → acids.
