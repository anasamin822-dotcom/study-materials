Module 1 - Introduction: Value Education

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers: why value education matters - the need, content, process, and the basic guidelines: happiness, prosperity and their continuity.

Key ideas: Value education teaches what is universally valuable for a human being, not just skills for a job. Every human wants two things: happiness (a state of harmony within) and prosperity (the feeling of having more than enough physical facilities) - continuously. The current crisis comes from chasing facilities while ignoring relationships and understanding. The unit introduces self-exploration as the process: verify proposals on your own right, in your own experience - don't accept them because a book says so, don't reject them without testing. Basic human aspiration = continuous happiness and prosperity; these come from right understanding + right feelings, not from money alone.

Exam pointers: definitions with one example each: value education, happiness, prosperity, self-exploration (with its two steps). "What is the need for value education in the present scenario?" is the standard long answer - answer it with the gap between facilities and fulfilment.

Module 2 - Harmony in the Human Being

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers: harmony in the human being - understanding the Self (I) and the Body as two co-existing realities, and what each needs.

Key ideas: A human being is Self (I) + Body. The Self is conscious - its needs are continuous happiness (feelings like trust, respect); the Body is material - its needs are physical facilities (food, shelter), needed only in limited quantity, temporarily. The activities differ too: the Self does imagination (desire, thought, expectation); the Body does recognising and fulfilling. Confusing the two - trying to get continuous happiness from physical facilities - is the root of dissatisfaction. The Self is the seer, doer and enjoyer; the Body is an instrument. Right understanding keeps the Self in charge, and the Body stays healthy as a result.

Exam pointers: "differentiate between the needs of the Self and the Body" in a table (continuous vs temporary, quantitative vs qualitative...) is the most repeated question. Also common: "human being as co-existence of Self and Body" and the four activities of the Self (imagination: desire, thought, expectation).

Module 3 - Harmony in the Family & Society

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers: harmony in the family and society - relationships, the nine feelings (values) in relationship, and the goal of an undivided society.

Key ideas: The family is the first place we live relationship. Relationship rests on feelings, not just physical exchange. The nine feelings: trust (foundation - being assured the other intends my happiness), respect (right evaluation - the other is similar to me), affection, care, guidance, reverence, glory, gratitude, love (complete - the feeling of being related to all). Justice = recognition, fulfilment and right evaluation in relationship, leading to mutual happiness. The societal goal: undivided society (akhand samaj) and universal human order (sarvabhauma vyavastha) built on five dimensions - education-sanskar, health-self-regulation, production-work, justice-preservation, exchange-storage.

Exam pointers: the nine feelings list (with trust and respect explained in 2-3 lines each) is asked again and again. "What is justice in human relationship?" and "goals of a universal human order (the five dimensions)" are the standard long answers.

Module 4 - Harmony in Nature & Existence

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers: harmony in nature and existence - the four orders of nature, their interconnectedness, and existence as co-existence.

Key ideas: Nature has four orders: material (soil, water, air), pranic/bio (plants), animal, and human. Each order has its own activity and innateness (svabhava): material order exists; pranic order lives and grows; animals live with will; humans alone have the capacity to know. The first three orders are already in harmony and mutually fulfilling - only the human order breaks the cycle by exploiting the rest. Existence = co-existence: units (everything we see) submerged in space (the all-pervading, no-activity reality). Everything is in space; space is the ground of all energy. Holistic perception means seeing this interdependence.

Exam pointers: "describe the four orders of nature with their innateness and activities" in a table is the guaranteed question. "Existence as co-existence (units submerged in space)" and "why is there a need for harmony in nature / recyclability and self-regulation" are the usual theory answers.

Module 5 - Harmony in Professional Ethics

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers: professional ethics - taking the harmony of the previous units into work and profession.

Key ideas: Professional ethics = conducting a profession with right understanding, so work serves human good, not just profit. Distinguish competence (skill) from character (values) - industry needs both. The five dimensions of human order map onto profession: production that enriches nature (not depletes it), work with responsibility, justice in dealings. Holistic technology is human-friendly, eco-friendly, and fits the real need. The unit also covers common contradictions: unethical practices (corruption, exploitation) arise from wrong goals; the answer is value-based profession and management. Competence in professional ethics = clarity about human goal + confidence in oneself + competence in mutually enriching production.

Exam pointers: "what is professional ethics / why is it needed today" and "competence in professional ethics (its three parts)" are the standard questions. Case-based answers score well when you connect the issue back to Self vs Body needs or the four orders - one concrete example lifts the answer.

QUESTION OF THE DAY

One exam question, every day

A new question rotates in each day - try it before you open the answer.

❓ List the nine feelings (values) in relationship. Which two are explained as the foundation and the base of evaluation?+
Trust, respect, affection, care, guidance, reverence, glory, gratitude, love. Trust (vishwas) is the FOUNDATION value; respect (sammaan) is right evaluation - seeing that the other is similar to me.
❓ Differentiate the needs of the Self (I) and the Body.+
Self needs are continuous and qualitative - happiness through feelings like trust and respect. Body needs are temporary and quantitative - physical facilities like food and shelter, needed in limited amounts. Mixing them up (seeking continuous happiness from facilities) causes dissatisfaction.
❓ Name the four orders of nature and the innateness (svabhava) of each.+
Material order (existence), pranic/plant order (existence + growth), animal order (existence, growth + will to live), human order (all of these + will to live with happiness / knowledge). The first three are naturally in harmony; humans must understand to participate.
❓ What is self-exploration (svatva)? Name its two steps.+
A process of dialogue within yourself - between 'what I am' and 'what I really want to be' (my natural acceptance). You verify each proposal on your own right, in your own living, instead of accepting or rejecting it blindly; what survives verification becomes your own understanding and you live by it.
CHEAT SHEET

Last-night revision

Key formulas and facts in one list - scan it the night before the paper.

  • Value education: understanding what is valuable (values) vs what is of use (skills) - both needed, values guide skills.
  • Basic human aspiration: continuous happiness (sukh) and prosperity (samriddhi).
  • Happiness = the state of being in harmony, within and with others; not momentary pleasure.
  • Prosperity = having more than enough physical facilities, and knowing it.
  • Right understanding + right feeling + physical facilities = human consciousness; animals live by assumption only.
  • The Self ('I') and the Body: Self has needs like trust/respect (continuous), Body needs food/shelter (temporary).
  • Sanyam (self-regulation) → Swasthya (health): the Self takes responsibility for the Body.
  • Harmony in family: justice - relationship based on trust (foundation) and respect (right evaluation).
  • Trust (vishwas): the assurance that the other intends my good. Respect = evaluating the other as they are.
  • Harmony in society: universal human order - undivided society (akhand samaj) with five dimensions: education, health, production, justice, exchange.
  • Nature's four orders: physical (soil/metal), bio (plants), animal, human - the first three are already in harmony; humans must understand it.
  • Existence = coexistence: units submerged in space; energy in equilibrium.
  • Professional ethics: using competence with humane values - competence guided by right understanding.
  • Svatantrata (self-organised) and Swarajya (self-rule): living by one's own natural acceptance, not external pressure.
