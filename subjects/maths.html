Unit 1 - Calculus I

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): limit, continuity and differentiability; Rolle's and mean-value theorems; Maclaurin/Taylor expansions (one and two variables); partial differentiation; maxima-minima and Lagrange multipliers.

Key ideas: A function is continuous at a point when its limit equals its value there; differentiable means the derivative exists (differentiable → continuous, not the reverse). Rolle's theorem: if f is continuous on [a,b], differentiable on (a,b), and f(a) = f(b), then some c has f'(c) = 0. Mean value theorem (Lagrange): f(b) - f(a) = f'(c)(b - a). Taylor/Maclaurin expand a function as an infinite polynomial - memorise the standard series: eˣ, sin x, cos x, log(1+x), (1+x)ᵏ. Partial derivatives differentiate with respect to one variable, treating the rest as constants. For maxima-minima in two variables: solve fx = 0, fy = 0, then check rt - s² (positive with r < 0 gives a maximum, positive with r > 0 a minimum). Lagrange multipliers handle constrained problems: solve ∇f = λ∇g.

Exam pointers: verify Rolle/LMVT for a given function (check the three conditions first - that is where marks hide), one Maclaurin expansion, and one maxima-minima of f(x,y) using the rt - s² test. Show every condition check explicitly.

Unit 2 - Calculus II

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): definite integrals and their properties, curve tracing, double and triple integrals, change of order and change of variables, Beta and Gamma functions.

Key ideas: Definite-integral properties save work: ∫ₐᵆ f = ∫ₐᵆ f(a+b-x), and ∫₋ᵄᵆ f = 2∫₀ᵆ f for even f (0 for odd f). Curve tracing: check symmetry, origin, intercepts, asymptotes, tangents at origin before sketching - list the steps in the answer. Double integrals compute area/volume: integrate inner first, keeping the outer variable fixed. Change of order: redraw the region from the limits and rewrite with axes swapped - the sketch is everything. Change of variables: multiply by the Jacobian |J| (polar: dx dy = r dr dθ). Beta/Gamma: Γ(n) = (n-1)!, Γ(1/2) = √π, and B(m,n) = Γ(m)Γ(n)/Γ(m+n) - they convert hard integrals into one-line answers.

Exam pointers: one "evaluate the double integral" (often needing order change - draw the region!), one Beta/Gamma evaluation using Γ(1/2) = √π, and a curve-tracing question are the regulars.

Unit 3 - Calculus III

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): Jacobians, approximation of errors, applications of definite integrals (surface areas and volumes of revolution), centre of mass and centre of gravity.

Key ideas: The Jacobian J = ∂(u,v)/∂(x,y) is the determinant of partial derivatives; it measures how much a transformation stretches area. Key facts: J·J' = 1 (for inverse transformations) and if u, v are functionally dependent then J = 0. Error approximation: for z = f(x,y), the approximate error δz ≈ (∂f/∂x)δx + (∂f/∂y)δy - used for "percentage error" problems. Applications of integrals: area under/between curves, volume of revolution V = π∫y²dx (about x-axis), and surface area of revolution. Centre of mass/gravity: x̄ = ∫x dm / ∫dm - for laminas use double integrals, for solids of revolution single integrals with density.

Exam pointers: compute a Jacobian (and test functional dependence), one error-approximation numerical (watch percentage-error wording), and a centre-of-gravity problem - the full worked example is in the notes under this unit.

Centre of gravity by integration
✎ Centre of Gravity by Integration - made simple (Instinct) - no video found, taught here+

Centre of gravity (CG) = the point where the whole weight feels balanced. Like the middle point of a balance scale.

Formulas (for a lamina/plate):

  • x̄ = (1/A) ∫∫ x dA   ,   ȳ = (1/A) ∫∫ y dA
  • where A = total area = ∫∫ dA

Steps: 1) Find the area A with a double integral. 2) Separately find ∫∫ x dA and ∫∫ y dA. 3) Divide each by A - done, you have (x̄, ȳ).

Think of tiny pizza slices. Add up each slice's "x contribution" (x dA), divide by the total area - you get the average x-position. That is the CG's x.

Exam tip: if the shape is symmetric (like a quadrant of a circle or a rectangle), the CG lies on the line of symmetry - half the work done.

Unit 4 - Vector Calculus

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): vectors and their differentiation; gradient, divergence, curl; directional derivative; line, surface and volume integrals; Green's, Stokes' and Gauss divergence theorems.

Key ideas: A scalar point function gives a number at each point (temperature); a vector point function gives a vector (velocity field). Gradient ∇φ points in the direction of fastest increase; its magnitude is the maximum rate. Directional derivative = ∇φ· (unit vector). Divergence ∇·F measures outflow per volume (source or sink); curl ∇xF measures rotation (curl ∇φ = 0 always). Integrals: line (along a curve), surface, volume. The three big theorems convert between them: Green's (line integral around a plane curve ↔ double integral over the region), Stokes' (line integral around a closed curve ↔ surface integral of curl), Gauss divergence (surface integral of F ↔ volume integral of div F).

Exam pointers: compute grad/div/curl of a given field (easy, mechanical marks), one directional-derivative numerical, and one theorem verification - Gauss divergence is the most asked. Remember curl grad = 0 and div curl = 0; they appear as 2-mark proofs.

Unit 5 - Matrices

📚 Unit notes - quick read before the videos+

What this unit covers (syllabus): matrices and their types, rank, consistency of linear systems, eigen values and eigen vectors, Cayley-Hamilton theorem, diagonalisation.

Key ideas: Know the types: symmetric (A = Aᵀ), skew-symmetric (A = -Aᵀ, diagonal zeros), orthogonal (AAᵀ = I), identity, singular (|A| = 0). Rank = number of non-zero rows after reducing to echelon form. For a system Ax = b: consistent (unique solution) when rank(A) = rank([A|b]) = number of unknowns; infinitely many when both ranks are equal but smaller; inconsistent when rank(A) < rank([A|b]). Eigen values come from |A - λI| = 0 (characteristic equation); for each λ, solve (A - λI)x = 0 for the eigen vector. Cayley-Hamilton: every matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation - use it to find A⁻¹ and higher powers. Properties: sum of eigen values = trace, product = determinant.

Exam pointers: the big repeated questions: find eigen values and vectors of a 3x3, verify Cayley-Hamilton and compute A⁻¹ from it, and test a system for consistency using ranks. Trace/determinant cross-checks catch arithmetic slips - always do them.

CHEAT SHEET

Last-night revision

Key formulas and facts in one list - scan it the night before the paper.

  • d/dx x^n = n·x^(n−1); d/dx e^x = e^x; d/dx ln x = 1/x.
  • d/dx sin x = cos x; d/dx cos x = −sin x; d/dx tan x = sec²x.
  • Product rule: (uv)' = u'v + uv'. Quotient rule: (u/v)' = (u'v − uv')/v². Chain rule: dy/dx = dy/du · du/dx.
  • ∫x^n dx = x^(n+1)/(n+1) + C (n ≠ −1); ∫(1/x)dx = ln|x| + C; ∫e^x dx = e^x + C.
  • ∫sin x dx = −cos x + C; ∫cos x dx = sin x + C; ∫sec²x dx = tan x + C.
  • Integration by parts: ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du - pick u by ILATE (Inverse, Log, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential).
  • Maxima/minima: f'(x) = 0 gives critical points; f''(x) < 0 → max, f''(x) > 0 → min.
  • Partial derivatives: differentiate treating other variables as constants.
  • Gradient ∇f = (fx, fy, fz) - direction of steepest increase; divergence = ∇·F, curl = ∇×F.
  • Dot product a·b = |a||b|cosθ; cross product |a×b| = |a||b|sinθ, perpendicular to both.
  • 2×2 inverse: for [[a,b],[c,d]], inverse = (1/det)[[d,−b],[−c,a]], det = ad − bc (must be non-zero).
  • Rank of a matrix = number of non-zero rows in echelon form.
  • System Ax = b: unique solution if rank(A) = rank([A|b]) = n; infinite if ranks equal but < n; none if unequal.
  • Eigenvalues: solve det(A − λI) = 0; eigenvectors from (A − λI)x = 0.
